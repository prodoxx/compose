### 0w. Write tickets a person can read at a glance
This applies to every ticket compose writes: epics, features, stories, tasks, design stories and tasks, and the GitHub issues the toolsmith files.
The reader is a person who was not in the conversation: the product owner, a reviewer, or a new engineer.
They should understand the ticket on the first read.
Write the way a good engineer writes a ticket for a teammate: direct, concrete, and plain.

**Title**
- Say what changes and where, in everyday words.
- Lead with the area (a screen, component, script, or command), then the change: `Score ring: show 20 colours and dim unlit blocks`.
- One change per title.
  If it needs "and" to join two changes, it is probably two tickets.
- Keep it under 80 characters; `compose linear create` and `update --title` refuse longer ones.
- Leave out exact values, internal names, and shorthand the reader has to decode (`18/26`, `Head padding`, `chain base`).
  Put them in the description.
- A bug title says what is broken, as the user sees it: `Job card: match label is unreadable on dark backgrounds`.

**Description**
- Open with one to three plain sentences: what is wrong or missing today, what changes, and why it matters.
  Someone who reads only this opening knows what the ticket is about.
- Then give the details the builder needs: exact names, values, files, and steps.
  Use lists and code formatting for them, and keep them out of the opening.
- Use short sentences, active voice, and one fact per sentence.
- Name things the way the product owner and the code name them, and explain a term the first time if the reader may not know it.
- No metaphors, wordplay, or compressed phrases that need a second read.

**Acceptance criteria**
- One observable result per bullet, written so anyone can check it.
- Start with the condition when there is one: `When the score is unknown, the ring shows "--" and all 20 blocks are dimmed.`
- Keep each bullet to one or two short sentences.
  Long exact values (SVG paths, colour lists, full commands) go in the description or the test plan, and the bullet points to them.
- Describe behavior, not implementation steps; the steps belong in the description.

**Before and after**
- Title before: `Draw the Job card's No score in muted foreground without the plain info icon`.
  After: `Job card: show "No score" in muted text, without the info icon`.
- Title before: `Make text-lg 18/26`.
  After: `Typography: set text-lg to 18px with a 26px line height`.
- Criterion before: `Unknown and pending draw all 20 blocks data-lit="false" with fill-score-segment-{i} and opacity-25, and "--".`
  After: `When the score is unknown or pending, all 20 blocks are dimmed and the ring shows "--".`
  The exact attributes go in the test plan.

Before you save a ticket, read it once as that reader.
Rewrite any sentence you had to read twice.
