### 0b. Shape the feature with the product owner
Shaping is the superpowers:brainstorming skill itself: invoke it (in Claude Code, the Skill tool with `superpowers:brainstorming`) and follow it as written.
Never paraphrase it here or anywhere in compose: the skill is the one source, so compose shapes exactly as it does, and a new version of the skill changes compose's shaping with it.
`compose setup` installs it and `compose doctor` requires it.
Its "human partner" is the product owner, and its "design doc" is the feature issue's design (The written design, below).
Compose changes only the points below; everything else, from "First: What Do You Actually Know?" to its Red Flags, is the skill's.

**What compose changes.**
- **Who.** The engineering manager holds the conversation: the session the product owner talks to, inside a terminal host (herdr, the Herdr app, Rex), and outside one the session they started; never a teammate, and never relayed through the team lead.
  Inside a terminal host the team lead asks the skill's "What do you actually know?" of a feature with no `## Intent`: when everything that matters is in the request, it writes the one-pass Intent (below); otherwise it reports `needs-user "shape <issue>: <what is missing>"` and waits (0h).
- **The quick, clear task.** The skill's "do it, and say what you did" is the one-pass Intent (below), and so is "just dive in" when the product owner opts out of the questions.
  The conversation runs whatever the request says when the issue carries the `needs-shaping` label, when a verification policy's globs match the files the feature will touch (the policy names the risk: a migration, billing, auth), or when the Intent would need a guess that is not easily reversible: money, data deleted, a user-facing commitment, scope the product owner settled elsewhere.
- **Look Before You Ask.** In a repo with code, the codebase brief (0e) is on the feature before the first message, and groundwork's toolchain findings (0g) join the conversation as they arrive.
  What Linear already decided is context the product owner gave: before any question, read the feature issue's Decisions so far and Notes, the description and comments of the feature, its epic, and the story at hand, and run `compose linear list --search "<terms>"`, scoped to the project in `.compose/linear.json`, for the question's key terms.
  A decision found there is settled: state it with its issue's name and link and build on it; when two recorded decisions conflict, name both and ask which one stands.
  A research look that is bigger than a few reads is a helper teammate (no `--cwd`), briefed with the question, what you know, and where to look; it reports once and your next message says what it found.
- **Decisions.** Every decision the product owner makes is recorded at once with their words (Recording decisions, below), and each playback chunk they accept goes at once into the design section it belongs to, so the issue always holds where the brainstorm is and a stopped session resumes from it.
- **The design doc.** It is never a file in the repo: it is written to the feature issue as the sections of The written design (below), short or full as the skill sizes it, and its approval is recorded with `compose decide` (The hand-off).
  A file the skill would write (notes, research findings) is a Linear document instead.
- **Builder check.** The skill's subagent is `compose buildercheck` (The builder check, below).
- **Visual companion.** Pictures go in Frame0 wireframes or on a Lavish page (Show, don't tell, below), never the skill's own browser server.
- **Spikes.** A spike is code on a throwaway branch in the project's worktree, never merged, with no plan, task, verify, or review.
- **After approval.** The next step is the planner (step 1; The hand-off, below), never superpowers:writing-plans, and nothing is built in this session.
- **Words.** Never use compose's process words with the product owner (map, question id, step, teammate).

**The one-pass Intent.**
Write `## Intent` (it opens with the user story sentence, then what it is for and why and who it serves), `## Acceptance criteria` (what done looks like, as numbered Given/When/Then scenarios), `## Constraints`, `## My calls` (every choice you made, one line each, so they can check it while reading), and `## Left to the builder` to the feature issue, one `compose linear update <id> --section "## <heading>" --file <path>` write each; `## Decided by the product owner` names only decisions already recorded.
Write them, and the feature's title, the way sections/0w.md says: plain words, the point first.
Intent and Acceptance criteria are the part product managers, designers and testers read; the CLI puts the other three under the issue's planning and decision log (0w).
A call that is not easily reversible is not yours: it is a reason to hold the conversation (above).
Every other call is a ruling, recorded with `compose decide --project <slug> --ruling --new "<decision>"` and named `ruled:` in the first report, which also says the Intent is on the issue.
Groundwork's toolchain findings (0g; which version, which option) become rulings the same way as they arrive, never a second wave.
No builder check and no approval: hand off as below, and a change the product owner asks for once anything is dispatched is a change of course (step 8).
A loose idea bigger than one feature is an epic: write its short design the same way (intent, goals, non-goals, decisions, the features it names), then each feature's Intent.

**Layers, only as deep as the next piece of work needs:**
- an epic only when the work is bigger than one feature: a short design on the epic (intent, goals, non-goals, decisions) that names its features, each then shaped on its own
- the feature, always: its written design, below
- stories, always, cut by the planner (step 1) from the approved design, thinnest end-to-end slice first; the engineering manager plays the planner's story proposal back to the product owner as 0h says
- a technical deep dive only for a story or epic that carries the label for it

A story or epic that needs a technical deep dive carries the Linear label `deep-dive`; propose it when a story's how is not obvious, and the product owner may add or remove it.
You run a deep dive yourself with the product owner, as you run this brainstorm, never a teammate.
A deep dive is a brainstorm on the technical side, and its artifact is a Linear document titled `<issue id>: tech doc`, linked from the issue under `## Tech doc`, read by a builder check like any design.
Write it with `compose linear doc create --title "<issue id>: tech doc" --file <path>`; the builder check that reads it is `compose buildercheck --project <slug> --issue <id>`.
After it, put the url it printed under the issue's `## Tech doc` with `compose linear update <id> --section "## Tech doc" --file <path>`.
The product owner approves the tech doc, and that is recorded the same way: `compose decide --project <slug> --issue <id> --new "the tech doc on <id> is approved" --quote "<their words>"`.
An approval takes the `Not shaped yet` placeholder off the issue, and `Needs a deep dive` with it once the tech doc is linked.
The `deep-dive` label stays on after the deep dive: the planner and readiness read the tech doc only where the label is, so a finished deep dive is the linked tech doc, never a dropped label.
A decision recorded on the issue later may outdate its tech doc, and `compose decide` says so as it records one.
Reread the doc then, and update it with `compose linear doc update`, or record that it stands: `compose decide --project <slug> --issue <id> --ruling --new "the tech doc on <id> stands after <D id>"`.
Readiness holds the issue's stories until one of the two is done.
Without the label, a story goes straight to the planner.

A story the readiness check judged not ready carries its questions under `## Open questions`, each tagged `(readiness check, <date>)`.
Planning that story starts from those questions, asked one at a time like any other, and each answer goes into the story itself, never only into the conversation.
Remove a question's line once its answer is on the story; the next check judges the story again.

**The written design.**
The design is written to the feature issue's description: Intent, Goals, Non-goals, Anti-goals, Acceptance criteria, Constraints, Approaches, Decided by the product owner, My calls, and Left to the builder.
Each is a `## ` section of that name, saved with `compose linear update <id> --section "## <heading>" --file <path>`, one write per section, never a file in the repo; an epic's design goes on the epic the same way.
Intent, Goals, Non-goals, Anti-goals and Acceptance criteria are the part people outside the build read, so they hold no file names, code or process words; the CLI puts the rest under the issue's planning and decision log (0w).
Intent opens with the user story sentence and says what it is for and why; Acceptance criteria are its numbered Given/When/Then scenarios (0w); Constraints says what it must live within; Approaches lists the two or three ways weighed, what each trades off, which one was picked, and why; My calls lists every choice you made for them, so they can check it while reading; Left to the builder names what the planner and implementers may choose.
Decided by the product owner names each decision by its `D<n>` id in `## Decisions so far`, where `compose decide` keeps it with their words; never retype a decision or its quote.
A **file** a skill would write (brainstorm notes, research findings) is a **Linear document** in the repo's project instead (`compose linear doc create --title "<issue id>: <purpose>" --file <path>`, linked from the design), never a file in the repo.

**The builder check.**
Before the product owner sees the design, a builder check reads it cold: `compose buildercheck --project <slug> --issue <id> | compose crew start --project <slug> --role builder-check --title "builder check" --brief -`.
It is a fresh reader with no one to ask, and it reports once, reaching you through your watch as a report from `<slug>-builder-check`: `done "<n> questions: ..."` or `done "nothing important left"`.
Outside herdr, give the printed brief to a fresh harness subagent with no conversation context and read its one report.
Its report is the builder's questions the skill sorts into its three piles.

**Recording decisions.**
Every decision the product owner makes is recorded at once with their words: `compose decide --project <slug> --new "<decision>" --quote "<their words>"` (`--issue <id>` when it is not the project's feature).
An answer to a question a teammate wrote is recorded the same way, with `--closes <id>` for each question it settles: one rule for every decision.
The quote is their message, verbatim; several decisions from one message go in one call, one `--new` each.
The command writes each as the next `D<n>` line under the issue's `## Decisions so far`, `D<n>: <decision> (product owner, <date>; said: "<their words>")`, and tells the team lead.
It refuses a decision without `--quote` unless away or yolo is on (below), and nobody writes a Decisions so far line by hand.
Before the product owner approves the design, a changed decision is recorded with `compose decide --project <slug> --new "<decision>" --replaces D<n> --quote "<their words>"`, which strikes the old line and adds the new D id.
After approval, a decision changes only through a change of course (step 8); `--replaces` refuses once stories exist.

**Show, don't tell.**
When the skill shows something, this is where it goes instead of its visual companion.
A **prototype** question about how a screen or flow should look is drawn in Frame0 through `compose frame0` commands (low-fi wireframes, one Frame0 page per question), shown in the project's Frame0 window on the product owner's screen, and named in the design by its page once they settle on it.
Nothing of Frame0 runs until a prototype question needs a wireframe: no session has `frame0` tools, and the app starts only when `compose frame0 up --project <slug>` is run, right before the first drawing, never at the start of a session on the chance that a wireframe will be needed.
Each project draws in its own Frame0 app on `.compose/<slug>/design.f0`, never a shared one: run `compose frame0 up --project <slug>` before the first drawing and `compose frame0 save --project <slug>` after each batch; a later session that runs `up` edits the same file.
The first `compose frame0` command after `up` makes Frame0 ask the product owner to allow "Frame0 MCP Server" (the name compose asks with): tell the product owner to click Allow, and retry once they say they did; if the command still fails, say so and wait.
Draw each page with one `compose frame0 draw --project <slug> --file <ops.json>`: a JSON list of tool calls in frame0-mcp-server's names and arguments, `{"tool": "create_text", "as": "title", ...its arguments}`, where `"$title"` in a later id argument is that shape's id.
It prints one line per shape with its id and box and saves the file, so a page costs one turn, not one per shape; a one-op list is for one fix.
To see a page yourself, export it with `compose frame0 export --project <slug> --page <page>` and read the PNG it writes.
Export a page once drawn, and again only after it changed.
`compose frame0 pages|move|delete` list and tidy its pages; never `curl` Frame0's API.
`teardown` saves and quits the project's Frame0.
Logic or stub prototypes are spikes (above).
Side-by-side designs or a mockup to click go on one Lavish page at `.compose/<slug>/lavish/shape.html` (ignored with the rest of `.compose/`, never committed), built with Lavish's design rules (`npx -y lavish-axi design`: the project's own design system if it has one, else the Lavish-recommended Tailwind and DaisyUI CDN) for its look only.
The page shows only the question being asked now: its id and its one-line question, then its pictures, each labeled with the option it shows.
Nothing else goes on it: no question already answered, no decision already settled, and no recap, summary, plan, or context section, whatever Lavish's playbooks suggest.
Write the page again from an empty file for each visual question, never by editing the last one, so nothing settled carries over; when no visual question is open, end it with `npx -y lavish-axi end <file>`.
Open it with `npx -y lavish-axi <file>` and give the product owner the `url:` it printed, copied and never retyped; they answer here in the conversation, so the page carries no answer controls.

**Away and yolo.**
Under away, a brainstorm waits for the product owner and is listed in the digest; under yolo, the engineering manager takes its own recommendation per question, tagged unconfirmed.
Under away, nothing of it is answered by recommendation: shaping stops where it is, and other ready work goes on.
Under yolo, each recommendation is recorded with `compose decide --project <slug> --new "<decision>"` and no `--quote`, which tags the line `(yolo recommendation, unconfirmed, <date>)` and logs it for the digest; the product owner may still steer, and their answer wins.
Under yolo, approving the design is one more recommendation: the theirs pile gets the recommendation the same way, the approval is recorded as below without `--quote`, and the digest lists it for the product owner to confirm.
When the product owner cannot answer now (a harness note says so, or no reply comes): leave the accepted playback on the issue, write the next question on the feature issue under `## Open questions` as well as saying it here, and stop there; 0r resumes it from the issue, and the section is emptied once its questions are answered.

**The hand-off.**
On the default path the planner (step 1) starts as soon as the Intent is on the issue: inside a terminal host the team lead wrote it and starts the planner itself; outside one, go on to step 1 in this session.
After the conversation, record the approval in their words: `compose decide --project <slug> --new "the design on <id> is approved" --quote "<their words>"`.
When the issue carries the `needs-shaping` label, drop it in the same step: `compose linear update <id> --drop-label needs-shaping`.
Inside a terminal host, tell the team lead with `compose crew prompt --project <slug> --role orchestrator "shaped <id>"`; outside one, go on to step 1 in this session.
After a conversation, the planner starts only once the product owner approves the design.
